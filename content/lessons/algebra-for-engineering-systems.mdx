# Algebra for Engineering Systems

## Objective

Use algebra to represent physical relationships, solve for unknown quantities, and check whether an engineering result is reasonable.

## Why It Matters

Every robot, drone, circuit, and simulation is full of relationships between quantities. Algebra turns those relationships into tools you can inspect and reuse.

## Concept

An equation is a compact model. If a sensor produces a voltage proportional to distance, or a battery runtime depends on current draw, algebra lets you rearrange the model and solve for the unknown.

For a linear relationship:

$$y = mx + b$$

`m` is the rate of change and `b` is the starting offset. In a technical system, the units matter as much as the numbers.

## Worked Example

A sensor reads `0.5 V` at zero distance and increases by `0.02 V` per centimeter:

$$V = 0.02d + 0.5$$

If the sensor reads `1.7 V`, solve for `d`:

$$1.7 = 0.02d + 0.5$$

$$1.2 = 0.02d$$

$$d = 60 cm$$

## Exercise

A motor controller maps throttle percentage `t` to wheel speed `s` with:

$$s = 0.12t + 0.4$$

Find the speed when `t = 50`, then solve for the throttle needed to reach `8.8 m/s`.
