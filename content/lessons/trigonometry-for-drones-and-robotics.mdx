# Trigonometry for Drones and Robotics

## Objective

Use sine, cosine, and tangent to convert between angles, distances, heights, and vector components.

## Why It Matters

Drones estimate altitude, camera footprint, bearing, and field of view with triangles. Robots decompose motion into forward, sideways, and vertical components.

## Concept

For a right triangle:

$$\sin(\theta) = \frac{opposite}{hypotenuse}$$

$$\cos(\theta) = \frac{adjacent}{hypotenuse}$$

$$\tan(\theta) = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}$$

Vector components use the same relationships:

$$x = r\cos(\theta)$$

$$y = r\sin(\theta)$$

## Worked Example

A drone points a camera downward with a horizontal field-of-view half-angle of `35°`. At `50 m` altitude, the half-width of the ground footprint is:

$$w = 50\tan(35^\circ)$$

$$w \approx 35.0m$$

The full footprint width is about `70 m`.
