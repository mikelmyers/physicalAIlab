# Radians and Arc Length

## Re-entry Promise

This lesson assumes you know circles, the unit circle, basic trig, and unit conversion. It does not assume that radians feel natural yet.

Degrees are familiar because people divided a circle into 360 pieces long ago. Radians are different. A radian is not an arbitrary slice of a circle. A radian connects angle directly to distance traveled along an arc.

The definition is:

$$
\theta=\frac{s}{r}
$$

where:

- $\theta$ is angle in radians,
- $s$ is arc length,
- $r$ is radius.

That one equation is why radians dominate calculus, physics, robotics, controls, circular motion, motor rotation, encoder math, and simulation.

By the end, you should be able to convert degrees and radians, compute arc length and sector area, connect angular velocity to linear velocity, and explain why radians are the natural unit for rotation.

## Objective

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- Define one radian using arc length and radius.
- Convert between degrees and radians.
- Compute arc length using $s=r\theta$.
- Compute sector area using $A=\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
- Explain why radians are dimensionless but not meaningless.
- Use angular velocity to compute linear speed.
- Interpret revolutions, radians, and encoder counts.
- Avoid common errors: using degrees in radian formulas, forgetting the half-angle in sectors, using diameter instead of radius, and rounding $\pi$ too early.

## Why It Matters

Radians are the language of rotation in real systems.

1. **Wheel odometry.** A wheel's angular rotation becomes linear travel by arc length.

2. **Motors and encoders.** Encoder counts measure fractions of a revolution. Control code often converts those counts into radians.

3. **Robot arms.** Joint angles are usually represented in radians in robotics software.

4. **Drones and IMUs.** Angular velocity, yaw rate, roll rate, and pitch rate are commonly measured in radians per second.

5. **Physics.** Circular motion, torque, angular momentum, waves, oscillation, and calculus use radians because the formulas become clean.

6. **Signal processing.** Frequency and phase are commonly written in radians.

Radians are not "hard degrees." They are angle measured in the unit that preserves geometry.

## Why This Idea Exists

On any circle, the same central angle sweeps a longer arc when the radius is larger. If the radius doubles, the arc length doubles. So the ratio:

$$
\frac{s}{r}
$$

removes the size of the circle and leaves only angle.

That ratio is the radian measure.

If $s=r$, then:

$$
\theta=\frac{r}{r}=1
$$

The angle is one radian.

So one radian is the central angle that cuts off an arc length equal to the radius.

## Full Rotation

A full circle has circumference:

$$
C=2\pi r
$$

Using $\theta=s/r$, the angle for a full rotation is:

$$
\theta=\frac{2\pi r}{r}=2\pi
$$

So:

$$
360^\circ=2\pi \text{ radians}
$$

That gives the conversion anchors:

$$
180^\circ=\pi \text{ radians}
$$

$$
90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2} \text{ radians}
$$

$$
1 \text{ revolution}=2\pi \text{ radians}
$$

## Conversion Formulas

Degrees to radians:

$$
\text{radians}=\text{degrees}\cdot\frac{\pi}{180}
$$

Radians to degrees:

$$
\text{degrees}=\text{radians}\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}
$$

Examples:

$$
30^\circ=30\cdot\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{\pi}{6}
$$

$$
45^\circ=\frac{\pi}{4}
$$

$$
60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}
$$

$$
\frac{3\pi}{2}\text{ radians}=270^\circ
$$

## Arc Length

The radian definition:

$$
\theta=\frac{s}{r}
$$

rearranges to:

$$
s=r\theta
$$

This formula requires $\theta$ in radians.

If $\theta$ is in degrees, convert first.

### Example: Arc Length

A robot arm of length 0.8 m rotates through $30^\circ$. How far does the gripper travel along its circular path?

Convert:

$$
30^\circ=\frac{\pi}{6}
$$

Then:

$$
s=r\theta=0.8\cdot\frac{\pi}{6}\approx0.419
$$

The gripper travels about 0.419 m along the arc.

## Sector Area

A sector is a slice of a circle.

The full circle area is:

$$
\pi r^2
$$

A full rotation is:

$$
2\pi
$$

So a sector with angle $\theta$ radians is the fraction:

$$
\frac{\theta}{2\pi}
$$

of the full circle.

Sector area:

$$
A=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\cdot\pi r^2=\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta
$$

Again, $\theta$ must be in radians.

### Example: Sector Area

A sensor covers a circular sector of radius 5 m and angle 0.8 radians.

$$
A=\frac12 r^2\theta=\frac12(25)(0.8)=10
$$

The coverage area is 10 m^2.

## Angular Velocity And Linear Speed

Angular velocity measures how fast an angle changes:

$$
\omega=\frac{d\theta}{dt}
$$

Its unit is usually radians per second.

Linear speed along a circular path is:

$$
v=r\omega
$$

This is the rate version of:

$$
s=r\theta
$$

### Example: Wheel Speed

A wheel has radius 0.3 m and spins at 10 rad/s.

$$
v=r\omega=0.3(10)=3
$$

The edge of the wheel moves at 3 m/s. If the wheel rolls without slipping, the vehicle moves at 3 m/s.

## Revolutions, RPM, And Encoders

One revolution is $2\pi$ radians.

If a motor turns at 120 RPM, that is:

$$
120 \frac{\text{rev}}{\text{min}}\cdot\frac{2\pi \text{ rad}}{1\text{ rev}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ min}}{60\text{ s}}=4\pi \text{ rad/s}
$$

Encoder counts work the same way. If an encoder has 2048 counts per revolution, then one count is:

$$
\frac{2\pi}{2048}\text{ rad}
$$

For a wheel radius $r$, distance per count is:

$$
r\cdot\frac{2\pi}{2048}
$$

This is why radians sit directly underneath robot odometry.

## Why Radians Are Dimensionless

Radians are defined as:

$$
\theta=\frac{s}{r}
$$

Both $s$ and $r$ are lengths. Meters divided by meters cancels units, so radians are technically dimensionless.

But dimensionless does not mean meaningless. A radian records a geometric ratio: arc length per radius.

In formulas, radians often disappear from unit analysis, but the angle interpretation remains essential.

## Common Failure Modes

1. **Using degrees in $s=r\theta$.** Convert degrees to radians first.
2. **Using diameter instead of radius.**
3. **Forgetting sector area has $\frac12$.**
4. **Treating radians as arbitrary.** They come from arc length divided by radius.
5. **Rounding $\pi$ too early.**
6. **Confusing angular speed with linear speed.** $\omega$ is rad/s; $v$ is m/s.
7. **Forgetting one revolution is $2\pi$, not $\pi$.**
8. **Ignoring wheel slip.** Odometry formulas assume rolling without slipping.
9. **Confusing RPM and rad/s.**
10. **Thinking dimensionless means unit-free in practice.** You still need to know the quantity is an angle.
