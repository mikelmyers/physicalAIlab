# Trigonometric Identities

## Re-entry Promise

This lesson assumes you know right-triangle trigonometry, the unit circle, radians, and basic algebra. It does not assume you already feel comfortable simplifying trigonometric expressions.

An **identity** is an equation that is true for every value where both sides are defined. Trigonometric identities are not random formulas to memorize. They are consequences of geometry, especially the unit circle.

The most important identity is:

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
$$

That is not magic. It is the Pythagorean theorem applied to the unit circle.

By the end, you should be able to use Pythagorean, reciprocal, quotient, cofunction, even-odd, and angle-sum identities to simplify expressions, verify equations, and recognize the trig structure that appears in physics, robotics, waves, and signal processing.

## Objective

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

- Explain what an identity is.
- Derive $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ from the unit circle.
- Use reciprocal identities for secant, cosecant, and cotangent.
- Use quotient identities for tangent and cotangent.

- Use even-odd identities to handle negative angles.

- Use angle-sum and angle-difference identities.
- Use double-angle identities in simple cases.
- Verify identities without making invalid algebraic moves.
- Apply identities to components, waves, AC signals, and rotations.

## Why It Matters

Trig identities are compression tools. They let you rewrite one form of a relationship into another form that is easier to compute, prove, model, or interpret.

1. **Physics.** Energy in simple harmonic motion often uses $\sin^2$ and $\cos^2$. The Pythagorean identity explains why total energy can remain constant while position and velocity trade off.

2. **Signal processing.** Products and sums of trig functions are rewritten to analyze frequency content.

3. **AC circuits.** Phase shifts and sinusoidal steady-state analysis use identities constantly.

4. **Robotics.** Rotation matrices rely on sine and cosine relationships. The fact that $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$ preserves vector length during rotation.

5. **Control systems.** Oscillation, phase, and feedback signals often become easier after trig rewriting.

6. **Calculus.** Derivatives and integrals of trig functions often require identities before they become manageable.

Identities are not a side quest. They are a way to keep complicated systems from turning into algebraic mud.

## Why This Idea Exists

The unit circle gives every angle a point:

$$
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
$$

Every point on the unit circle satisfies:

$$
x^2+y^2=1
$$

Substitute $x=\cos\theta$ and $y=\sin\theta$:

$$
\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1
$$

That is the foundational trig identity.

Other identities come from the same kind of structure: ratios, symmetry, complementary angles, and rotation. They look like algebra, but they are geometry wearing symbols.

## What An Identity Is

An equation like:

$$
2x+3=11
$$

is true only for one value: $x=4$.

An equation like:

$$
2(x+3)=2x+6
$$

is true for every $x$. That is an identity.

A trig identity is true for every angle where the expressions are defined:

$$
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
$$

This is true whenever $\cos\theta\ne0$.

The domain phrase matters. If an expression divides by zero at some angles, the identity is not defined there.

## Core Identities

### Pythagorean Identity

The base identity is:

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
$$

This can be rearranged:

$$
\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta
$$

$$
\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta
$$

Do not read $\sin^2\theta$ as $\sin(\theta^2)$. It means:

$$
(\sin\theta)^2
$$

### Quotient Identities

Tangent is sine divided by cosine:

$$
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
$$

Cotangent is cosine divided by sine:

$$
\cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}
$$

These are useful when an expression mixes tangent with sine and cosine.

### Reciprocal Identities

The reciprocal trig functions are:

$$
\sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}
$$

$$
\csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}
$$

$$
\cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta}
$$

They are less common in engineering code than sine, cosine, and tangent, but they appear frequently in algebra, calculus, and symbolic manipulation.

### Tangent-Secant Identity

Start with:

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
$$

Divide every term by $\cos^2\theta$:

$$
\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}+\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}=\frac{1}{\cos^2\theta}
$$

So:

$$
\tan^2\theta+1=\sec^2\theta
$$

Usually written:

$$
1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta
$$

### Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

Divide the Pythagorean identity by $\sin^2\theta$:

$$
1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta
$$

These identities become important in calculus and when manipulating slopes and reciprocal ratios.

## Symmetry Identities

### Even And Odd

Cosine is even:

$$
\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta
$$

Sine is odd:

$$
\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta
$$

Tangent is odd:

$$
\tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta
$$

On the unit circle, negative angles reflect across the $x$ axis. The $x$ coordinate stays the same, so cosine stays the same. The $y$ coordinate changes sign, so sine changes sign.

### Cofunction Identities

Sine and cosine swap on complementary angles:

$$
\sin(90^\circ-\theta)=\cos\theta
$$

$$
\cos(90^\circ-\theta)=\sin\theta
$$

In radians:

$$
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\cos\theta
$$

$$
\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\sin\theta
$$

This comes from the two acute angles in a right triangle adding to $90^\circ$.

## Angle-Sum And Difference Identities

These are essential for rotation, phase shifts, waves, and proving deeper facts.

$$
\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b
$$

$$
\sin(a-b)=\sin a\cos b-\cos a\sin b
$$

$$
\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b
$$

$$
\cos(a-b)=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b
$$

Notice that:

$$
\sin(a+b)\ne\sin a+\sin b
$$

That false move is extremely common.

### Example: Exact Value For 75 Degrees

Since:

$$
75^\circ=45^\circ+30^\circ
$$

Use:

$$
\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b
$$

$$
\sin(75^\circ)=\sin(45^\circ)\cos(30^\circ)+\cos(45^\circ)\sin(30^\circ)
$$

$$
=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12
$$

$$
=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}
$$

## Double-Angle Identities

Double-angle identities come from setting $a=b=\theta$ in the angle-sum identities.

$$
\sin(2\theta)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta
$$

$$
\cos(2\theta)=\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta
$$

Using the Pythagorean identity, cosine's double-angle identity can also be written:

$$
\cos(2\theta)=1-2\sin^2\theta
$$

or:

$$
\cos(2\theta)=2\cos^2\theta-1
$$

These forms are useful in signals and calculus because they can rewrite squared trig functions or products.

## Verifying Identities

To verify an identity, transform one side until it matches the other side. Usually, start with the more complicated side.

Good moves:

- Replace $\tan\theta$ with $\sin\theta/\cos\theta$.
- Replace $\sec\theta$ with $1/\cos\theta$.
- Use $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.
- Factor, combine fractions, or multiply by a useful form of 1.
- Keep domain restrictions in mind.

Risky moves:

- Dividing by an expression that might be zero without noting restrictions.
- Squaring both sides, which can introduce false solutions.
- Moving pieces across both sides until you accidentally prove only a conditional equation.
- Assuming the identity is true and working in circles.

### Example: Verify $1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta$

Start from the Pythagorean identity:

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
$$

Subtract $\sin^2\theta$ from both sides:

$$
\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta
$$

So:

$$
1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta
$$

### Example: Simplify $\tan\theta\cos\theta$

Use:

$$
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
$$

Then:

$$
\tan\theta\cos\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\cos\theta=\sin\theta
$$

This simplification is valid where $\cos\theta\ne0$, because tangent was not defined there anyway.

## Engineering Connections

### Rotation Preserves Length

A 2D rotation uses:

$$
x'=x\cos\theta-y\sin\theta
$$

$$
y'=x\sin\theta+y\cos\theta
$$

The identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ is part of why rotation changes direction without changing length.

### Phase Shifts

A signal like:

$$
\sin(t+\phi)
$$

can be expanded:

$$
\sin t\cos\phi+\cos t\sin\phi
$$

That means a shifted sine wave can be represented as a combination of sine and cosine. This matters in signal processing and AC circuit analysis.

### Oscillation Energy

If position varies like cosine and velocity varies like sine, squared terms often add to a constant. That is the Pythagorean identity appearing as conservation structure.

## Common Failure Modes

1. **Thinking identities are equations to solve.** Identities are true across a domain; equations may be true only for specific values.
2. **Writing $\sin(a+b)=\sin a+\sin b$.** This is false.
3. **Forgetting domains.** Tangent, secant, cotangent, and cosecant are not defined everywhere.
4. **Confusing $\sin^2\theta$ with $\sin(\theta^2)$.**
5. **Dividing by a possibly zero expression.**
6. **Squaring both sides carelessly.**
7. **Memorizing without geometry.** The Pythagorean identity is unit-circle geometry.
8. **Dropping negative signs in quadrant or even-odd identities.**
9. **Using degree cofunction identities while working in radians without conversion.**
10. **Trying random transformations.** Start with known identities and the more complicated side.
