# Arithmetic, Units, and Estimation

## Objective

Rebuild practical number sense: add, subtract, multiply, divide, track units, and estimate whether an answer is reasonable.

## Why It Matters

Engineering errors often start before the advanced math. A misplaced decimal, missing unit, or unreasonable estimate can break a circuit, crash a drone, or make a simulation meaningless.

## Concept

Arithmetic is not just calculation. It is controlled thinking about quantities.

A quantity has two parts:

$$quantity = number \times unit$$

For example, `12 V`, `3.5 A`, `40 m`, and `2 hours` all combine a number with a unit. The unit tells you what kind of thing the number measures.

Estimation is the habit of asking: "What size should this answer be?"

If a drone flies `10 m/s` for `6 s`, the distance should be around:

$$10 \times 6 = 60m$$

If a calculator says `6000 m`, something went wrong.

## Worked Example

A small robot uses `0.8 A` while driving. It drives for `15 minutes`. How much charge does it use in amp-hours?

First convert minutes to hours:

$$15 min = \frac{15}{60} hr = 0.25 hr$$

Then multiply current by time:

$$charge = 0.8A \times 0.25hr = 0.2Ah$$

The answer is `0.2 Ah`.
