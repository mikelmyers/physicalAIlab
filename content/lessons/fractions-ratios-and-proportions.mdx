# Fractions, Ratios, and Proportions

## Re-entry Promise

This lesson assumes fractions may feel rusty. We will not treat that as a weakness. Fractions, ratios, rates, and proportions are some of the most practical ideas in all of technical work, so we are going to rebuild them carefully.

You do not need to memorize tricks. You need to understand what is being compared.

## Objective

By the end, you should be able to:

- Explain fractions as part-whole relationships and division.
- Explain ratios as comparisons between quantities.
- Explain rates as ratios with different units.
- Use proportions to scale measurements and solve unknowns.
- Recognize proportional relationships in tables, graphs, formulas, and real systems.
- Apply ratios to engineering, circuits, robotics, drones, calibration, and maps.

## Why It Matters

Ratios are everywhere in the physical world:

- gear ratios,
- voltage dividers,
- map scale,
- drone image scale,
- camera aspect ratio,
- motor speed reduction,
- battery runtime,
- sensor calibration,
- unit conversion,
- mixture concentration,
- probability,
- slopes and rates of change.

If arithmetic is the language of quantity, ratios are the language of comparison.

## Why This Idea Exists

Humans needed ratios because raw counts were not enough.

If one machine uses `2 A` and another uses `4 A`, the second uses twice the current. That "twice" is a ratio.

If a map distance is `1 cm` and the real distance is `10 m`, the map is useful only because there is a stable relationship between drawing and reality.

If a gear turns three times for every one turn of an output shaft, the machine changes speed and torque through a ratio.

Fractions and ratios exist because builders, scientists, navigators, cooks, merchants, and engineers needed to compare parts, wholes, rates, and scales.

## Plain-Language Intuition

A fraction answers: "How many parts of a whole?"

$$\frac{3}{4}$$

means three out of four equal parts.

A ratio answers: "How does one quantity compare to another?"

$$3:4$$

means 3 of one thing for every 4 of another thing.

A rate answers: "How much of one kind of quantity happens per one unit of another?"

$$12\frac{meters}{second}$$

means 12 meters for every 1 second.

A proportion says two comparisons are equivalent:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

That is what makes scaling possible.

## Core Theory

### Fractions As Part Of A Whole

A fraction has a numerator and denominator:

$$\frac{numerator}{denominator}$$

The denominator says how many equal parts the whole is divided into.

The numerator says how many of those parts are being counted.

For example:

$$\frac{3}{8}$$

means 3 parts out of 8 equal parts.

### Fractions As Division

A fraction can also mean division:

$$\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8$$

This is why fractions can be converted to decimals:

$$\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$$

### Equivalent Fractions

Fractions can look different while representing the same value:

$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}$$

You can multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number without changing the value.

$$\frac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 3} = \frac{3}{6}$$

### Ratios

A ratio compares quantities.

If a robot gearbox has a `3:1` reduction, that can mean the motor turns 3 times for every 1 output turn.

Written as a fraction:

$$\frac{motor\ turns}{output\ turns} = \frac{3}{1}$$

Order matters. `3:1` is not the same statement as `1:3`.

### Part-Part And Part-Whole Ratios

A part-part ratio compares two parts:

`2 red wires : 3 black wires`

A part-whole ratio compares one part to the total:

$$\frac{2 red wires}{5 total wires}$$

Confusing these is a common mistake.

### Rates

A rate is a ratio comparing different units:

$$speed = \frac{distance}{time}$$

$$12\frac{m}{s}$$

means 12 meters per second.

Other rates:

- dollars per part,
- samples per second,
- rotations per minute,
- meters per pixel,
- amps per hour is usually a warning because battery capacity is normally amp-hours, not amps per hour.

### Unit Rates

A unit rate tells the amount per one unit.

If a robot travels `24 m` in `6 s`:

$$unit\ rate = \frac{24m}{6s} = 4\frac{m}{s}$$

That means 4 meters per 1 second.

### Proportions

A proportion is an equation that states two ratios are equal:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

If three values are known, the fourth can often be solved.

Example:

$$\frac{1cm}{5m} = \frac{8cm}{x}$$

This says 1 cm on a map corresponds to 5 m in reality, and 8 cm corresponds to an unknown real distance.

### Cross Products

For:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

the cross products are equal:

$$a \times d = b \times c$$

This works because multiplying both sides by `bd` clears the denominators.

But cross multiplication is not magic. It is just equation solving.

### Proportional Relationships

A proportional relationship has a constant multiplier:

$$y = kx$$

`k` is the constant of proportionality.

If `x` doubles, `y` doubles. If `x` is cut in half, `y` is cut in half.

On a graph, a directly proportional relationship is a straight line through the origin.

## Mental Models

### Double Number Line

For a map scale:

| Map distance | 1 cm | 2 cm | 4 cm | 8 cm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Real distance | 5 m | 10 m | 20 m | 40 m |

Each column preserves the same ratio.

### Ratio Table

For speed:

| Time | 1 s | 2 s | 3 s | 4 s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Distance | 4 m | 8 m | 12 m | 16 m |

The unit rate is `4 m/s`.

### Scaling

Scaling means multiplying every relevant measurement by the same factor.

If a drawing is doubled, a `3 cm` line becomes `6 cm`.

But be careful: area and volume scale differently than length.

If length doubles, area becomes four times larger:

$$2^2 = 4$$

If length doubles, volume becomes eight times larger:

$$2^3 = 8$$

This matters in mechanical design.

## Worked Example 1: Map Scale

A map uses:

$$1cm = 5m$$

A path is `8 cm` on the map. Find the real distance.

Set up the proportion:

$$\frac{1cm}{5m} = \frac{8cm}{x}$$

Use the scale factor:

$$8 \times 5m = 40m$$

The real path is `40 m`.

Reasonableness check: `8 cm` is 8 times the map unit, so the real distance should be 8 times `5 m`.

## Worked Example 2: Gear Ratio

A gearbox has a `4:1` reduction. The motor spins at `1200 RPM`. What is the output speed?

Interpretation:

The motor turns 4 times for every 1 output turn.

$$output = \frac{motor}{4}$$

$$output = \frac{1200RPM}{4} = 300RPM$$

The output shaft spins at `300 RPM`.

Engineering note: speed decreases, but torque often increases, ignoring losses.

## Worked Example 3: Voltage Divider Ratio

A voltage divider uses two resistors in series. The output ratio is:

$$\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

If `R1 = 1000 ohms` and `R2 = 1000 ohms`:

$$\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{1000}{1000 + 1000}$$

$$\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{1}{2}$$

If input is `5 V`, output is:

$$V_{out} = 2.5V$$

This is a ratio doing real electrical work.

## Worked Example 4: Drone Image Ground Scale

A drone image has a ground scale of `4 cm/pixel`. A feature is `250 pixels` long. Estimate its real length.

Convert:

$$4cm/pixel \times 250pixels = 1000cm$$

Convert centimeters to meters:

$$1000cm = 10m$$

The feature is about `10 m` long.

The pixels cancel, leaving distance.

## Worked Example 5: Part-Part vs Part-Whole

A kit has `2 motors` and `6 propellers`.

Part-part ratio:

$$motors:propellers = 2:6 = 1:3$$

There is 1 motor for every 3 propellers.

Part-whole fraction of motors:

$$\frac{2 motors}{8 total parts} = \frac{1}{4}$$

The ratio `1:3` and fraction `1/4` are both true, but they answer different questions.

## Common Failure Modes

### Failure Mode 1: Reversing The Ratio

`3 motor turns : 1 output turn` is not the same as `1 motor turn : 3 output turns`.

Fix: write labels on both parts of the ratio.

### Failure Mode 2: Mixing Part-Part And Part-Whole

If a kit has `2 motors` and `6 propellers`, the motor-to-propeller ratio is `1:3`, but motors are `1/4` of the total parts.

Fix: ask whether you are comparing part to part or part to whole.

### Failure Mode 3: Ignoring Units In Rates

`10 meters per second` and `10 seconds per meter` are completely different.

Fix: keep units in the fraction.

### Failure Mode 4: Assuming Everything Scales Linearly

Length, area, volume, mass, strength, heat, and power may scale differently.

Fix: identify what kind of quantity you are scaling.

## Engineering Connections

### Robotics

Gear ratios connect motor speed to output shaft speed. Wheel circumference converts rotations into distance.

### Electronics

Voltage dividers, resistor tolerances, duty cycles, and sensor calibration all use ratios.

### Drones And GIS

Map scale, camera field of view, pixel ground sample distance, and flight speed all depend on ratios and rates.

### Computer Vision

Camera images use aspect ratios, pixel coordinates, and scale factors to connect image measurements to real-world measurements.

### Physics

Rates such as velocity, acceleration, power, density, and frequency are ratio ideas. Calculus later studies changing ratios at a deeper level.
