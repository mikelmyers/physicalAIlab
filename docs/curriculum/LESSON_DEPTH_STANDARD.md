# Lesson Depth Standard

Physical AI Lab lessons are not short blog posts. They are study chapters for a learner rebuilding from first principles and working toward research-grade capability.

Every serious lesson should eventually include:

## 1. Re-entry Promise

State what the lesson assumes and what it does not assume. If the learner has not been in school for years, the lesson should still be approachable.

## 2. Plain-Language Intuition

Explain the idea before symbols. Use ordinary words, physical examples, and concrete quantities.

## 3. Why The Idea Exists

Explain the historical, practical, or scientific need that made the concept useful. The learner should know why humans bothered to invent or formalize it.

## 4. Core Theory

Give definitions, rules, notation, assumptions, and edge cases. Do not hide the real theory, but build up to it carefully.

## 5. Visual Or Mental Model

Include number lines, tables, coordinate sketches, graphs, diagrams, or clear written mental models.

## 6. Worked Examples

Include easy, medium, practical, and failure-mode examples. Each worked example should show the reasoning, not just the answer.

## 7. Engineering Connections

Connect the concept to robotics, circuits, drones, mechatronics, coding, telemetry, computer vision, or physics.

## 8. Practice Set

Include enough repetition to build fluency:

- warmups
- applied problems
- mixed review
- challenge problems
- error-spotting problems

## 9. Build Task

Turn the concept into an artifact: code, notebook, calculator, simulation, diagram, measurement, lab note, or public writeup.

## 10. Quiz

Ask conceptual and procedural questions. Avoid trivia. Check whether the learner understands why the procedure works.

## 11. Proof Of Completion

Define the artifact that proves the learner did the work. Proof beats passive reading.

## 12. Sources And Further Study

List source references and optional next resources. Sources should be authoritative when possible: standards bodies, open textbooks, university courses, or primary documentation.

## 13. Next Lesson Bridge

Explain how this lesson supports the next concept.

This standard is intentionally demanding. The curriculum can start with lighter seed lessons, but the long-term goal is to upgrade each essential lesson into this shape.
